Why it matters

Systems run alone.
Architecture makes them work together.

Modern organizations depend on many systems that must share data and work as one. Systems integration and architecture give you the methods to connect them reliably—balancing interoperability, security, performance, and change.

About this course

From separate systems to one working solution.

Systems Integration and Architecture 1 (IT PC 3) moves you beyond building isolated applications. You learn what systems and information are, how to gather and document requirements, how architecture shapes integration, and how a real integration project is run, from requirements gathering to maintenance.

Classroom policies

Guidelines for success in this course.

Please review and adhere to these policies to ensure a productive learning environment for everyone.

A. Attendance and Punctuality

  1. Students who will be absent must secure an admission slip from the College Guidance Coordinator and present it to the instructor before returning to class. Attach a medical certificate for illness-related absences.
  2. Excused absences for official university activities may qualify students to complete missed examinations, quizzes, and class activities.
  3. Absences exceeding 20% of required class hours may result in being dropped. If 60% or more are unexcused, the student may receive a grade of 5.00.
  4. Students must enter class within the first 15 minutes; later arrivals may be considered absent.

B. Quizzes and Examinations

  1. Two long examinations are scheduled during the semester: midterm and final examinations. Follow the Registrar's schedule.
  2. For a missed examination, secure an excuse slip and discuss rescheduling with the instructor.
  3. Quizzes may be announced or unannounced. Missed quizzes and activities follow the Student Handbook.

C. Uniform and Dress Code

  1. Uniform requirements are implemented according to the Student Handbook.

D. Other Classroom Rules

  1. Academic integrity: Submitted work must be original or clearly acknowledge others' work. Cheating, fabrication, plagiarism, sabotage, and other dishonest academic activities are subject to university rules.
  2. Responsible use of AI: Use clear prompts, verify facts and numbers, take ownership of the final work, and disclose or cite AI assistance according to APA Style 7th Edition when required. Use AI thoughtfully rather than relying on it entirely.
  3. Consultations: Visit the instructor during the stated consultation hours and avoid transactions during break time or lunch whenever possible. Be courteous to faculty and staff.
  4. Cleanliness and orderliness: Keep the classroom clean before and after class and practice CLAYGO (Clean As You Go).
